### Description

QuitNic gives you a quieter way through nicotine cravings.

When an urge arrives, open Rescue for an immediate guided breathing reset. Log
the trigger and outcome without completing a long form, then return to Today and
keep moving. Journey turns your nicotine-free time into a clear roadmap, while
Coach helps you reflect on patterns and prepare for difficult moments.

With QuitNic you can:

- start a breathing reset immediately when a craving hits
- record cravings and slips with a quick, focused check-in
- follow your nicotine-free time, estimated savings, and upcoming checkpoints
- review and correct previous check-ins
- receive behavioural coaching based on the context you choose to share
- continue using core tracking features while offline
- delete coaching history or your anonymous account from Settings

QuitNic supports quitting cigarettes, vaping, nicotine pouches, and other
nicotine products. It is designed without advertising or cross-app tracking.

QuitNic provides general behavioural support and educational information. It is
not medical care and does not diagnose, prescribe treatment, or replace a
qualified healthcare professional. Contact local emergency services if you may
be in immediate danger.

## App review information

### Sign-in

No reviewer account is required. The app creates a random anonymous device
account when network features are first used.

### Review notes

QuitNic is a nicotine-cessation support and habit-tracking app. Core tracking is
available offline. To exercise the main flow:

1. Complete onboarding with any non-empty motivation.
2. On Today, tap **Craving?** to open the guided Rescue flow.
3. Use **Quick Log** to record a craving without waiting for the breathing cycle.
4. Open **Journey** to review checkpoints.
5. Open **Coach** to send a behavioural-support message.
6. Open Settings → Delete data to test coaching-history or anonymous-account deletion.

The app does not provide diagnosis, medication dosing, or emergency services.
Optional cloud transcription is used only when the person explicitly chooses it.
